A route is suitable for a robot sharing space with people when it actually reaches the goal, keeps a comfortable distance from people without scraping against walls, and doesn't take more time than it needs to. Across the three missions, what the robot actually did mattered more than how efficient the route seemed by the planner. 

In Mission 1, search efficiency told me less than I expected. On the open map, BFS and A* both found a 13.5 m path, and A* expanded 700 cells against BFS’s 928. The planning time was about a millisecond for both, which is tiny next to the 35 to 90 seconds the robot spends driving. DFS showed that cheap search doesn’t mean a good route. On the constrained map it expanded only 598 cells compared with BFS’s 848, yet its path was 74.5 m instead of 24.5 m. IDDFS hit resource_limit on both maps, meaning it ran out of its expansion budget before finishing, so I can’t say whether it would have found a route. That differs from no_path on the unreachable map, where all four planners finished searching and proved there was no route.

Mission 2 showed that a good plan isn’t the same as a finished trip. I predicted BFS would beat DFS on the constrained map, and it did. BFS reached the goal in about 90 seconds while DFS timed out at 180 seconds, still 5.64 m away. BFS and A* behaved almost identically, with equal 13.5 m plans and elapsed times of 46.8 and 46.4 seconds on the open map, which fits both solving the same unit-cost graph. IDDFS returned no route on either map, so there was nothing to drive. The planners working in continuous space beat the grid optimum, with PRM fastest on the open map at 35.5 seconds and Nav2 fastest on the constrained map at 72.8 seconds. Nav2 still failed on the open map with a controller error, ending 0.29 m from the goal against a 0.25 m tolerance, so even the supplied baseline isn’t guaranteed.

In Mission 3, all six A* and RRT runs succeeded. RRT kept far more distance from the person, with clearance between 1.68 and 1.74 m against 0.72 to 0.73 m for A*, and it spent no time inside the personal space threshold while A* spent 1.3 to 1.6 seconds. The cost was that RRT passed within 0.14 to 0.18 m of walls while A* stayed about 0.6 m away. I would keep RRT social_1 for a scenario about people, but A* social_2 would be safer if walls mattered more,

Throughout, plans are estimates and driven distance is the outcome. Other planners beat the 13.5 m grid optimum, and BFS planned 24.5 m but drove 23.93 m. Lidar readings and AMCL position are the robot’s belief, not truth, and everything was simulated with static people, so it can’t show how the robot handles people who move or whether the spacing feels comfortable.
