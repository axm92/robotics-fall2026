# line_following

## comparison
On the straight practice strip, I used the starting settings where gain was 0.08, speed 0.16 and the robot just continued on straight down the magenta line with a few stops along the way. On the curved track, would drift far off from the center and seem to be a lot slower in completing the track. In my second track attempt I changed only the gain, from 0.08 to 0.2. This attempt stayed in TRACK for the whole run and reached COMPLETE at about 146.7 seconds, with no line loss. The error plot shows the line moving off center on the bends, reaching about +0.5 near 42 seconds and about -0.6 near 103 seconds, and then returning to near zero by the end. The turn command was always the opposite sign of the error and was about 0.2 times its size, so the robot steered back toward the line. The forward speed dropped from 0.16 to about 0.09 when the error was large. This supports the idea that a higher gain corrects bends better than the weak starting gain. There was some overshoot in the small ripples on each peak, but it did not grow, so the tracking stayed stable. I changed only one setting, so the improvement can be linked to the gain.

## limits
The stop in this attempt was the planned one. The controller entered COMPLETE at about 146.7 seconds, after it saw the red finish panel in three separate frames, and the speed and turn commands went to zero. It was not a failure that began in image processing or control, because the magenta line was detected the whole time. It does show a limit from missing information. The red panel is a simulation cue, so the robot stops because it recognizes a color, not because it knows how far it is from anything. A real delivery robot around people would need more sensing, such as lidar or depth cameras to see people and obstacles, bump sensors, and a separate emergency stop. It would also need tests in different lighting, with a worn or partly covered line, with people crossing its path, on wet floors, and with camera failures. Those tests should measure stopping distance and show the robot stops safely every time, and not only on a successful run.
