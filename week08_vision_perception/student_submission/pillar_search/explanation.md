## evidence
Red was centered, the blue and green was almost fully centered, while turquoise was off center. A centered colored region and a stable hold show only that some group of pixels matches a color rule and has stayed near the middle of the image for a while. They don’t prove object identity, because the detector doesn’t know what the object is. It only knows that pixels fall inside a hue, saturation and value range, and anything with a similar color can pass, such as a different object, a shadow, or a wooden desk, as we saw with the mug and its shadow. A stable hold doesn’t fix this, because a steady wrong region is still a wrong region. It only shows the result isn’t flickering from frame to frame. It also doesn’t prove distance. A colored region’s size and position in the image depend on the camera’s view and the object’s size, so a small region could be a small object up close or a large object far away, and the center of the image doesn’t say how far away something is. To measure distance you’d need other information, such as depth from a depth camera or stereo camera, the object’s known real size, or views from more than one angle.
