I learned a lot from this about visual evidence and how it relates to reliable robot behavior. Robots have only the knowledge we provide it with and test it with. The visual aspect of robots is key to how successful a robot design is. If there is adequate accurate vision from a robot it will make for a safer robot. I learned that testing all different types of visual evidence is important and how it should not be implied that a robot knows and can see everything in the way we can. For us, it is a lot easier to discern certain objects in relations to others and complete simple tasks, but for robots we must go the extra mile to get it anywhere near our level of vision. 

Across the three missions, I learned that a detector finding something is not the same as a robot knowing what to do, and that each step from image to action needs its own evidence. In the detector comparison, my classical color rule seemed fine on counts alone, since it reported a detection on every image where a mug was present, but the saved boxes told a different story. On normal_1, the winning region was contour 7 (13,533 px²), which merged the mug with its shadow, so the box center landed on the desk. In the condition table, the classical detector had 0 correct boxes and 2 false boxes in every condition, while the learned detector had 2 correct boxes in the normal images, with a tight box on the mug labeled cup. Dim light made both fail, but in different ways: in dim_1, the mug body dropped out of the raw mask, and the classical detector chose contour 18 (7,532 px²), mostly shadow and desk, while the learned detector drew no box at all. For a robot, that is an important difference, because a wrong box could send it to the wrong place and a missed detection is easier to notice. In the pillar centering mission, some of the pillars were more centered than others and it was easier to center ones who were in frame for longer so finding the right balance for this is important. The last mission, demonstrating how a robot can navigate a space with just a colored line and no knowledge of anything else was helpful to my overall understanding of ways robots can navigate different spaces.
